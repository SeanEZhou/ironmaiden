# Engineering log: design and training of the EDF humanoid

This is the deep-dive companion to the README. It records every design iteration and every training run, what changed, what happened, and what we learned, including the dead ends. Numbers come from `hardware/sizing.py` (design) and `evaluation/evaluate.py` (100 randomized trials, held-out seed 12345).

**Conventions**
- **E2** is the selected hardware design: 0.88 m, 10.74 kg.
- **vN** is a policy version.
- **DR** means domain randomization.
- **"Strict"** means the spec criterion as finally enforced.
- Bold rows are reference results.

---

## Part A: Design phase

### A1. Approach

The order of work was fixed by the spec's M0 gate: **no training until a purchasable configuration closes on mass, thrust, power, torque and controllability.** The design is fully parametric:
- **`models/design.py`** turns height plus a part selection into geometry and a list of massed components.
- **`models/build_mjcf.py`** turns that list into the MuJoCo model, so mass, COM and inertia come from the physics engine, not a spreadsheet.
- **`hardware/sizing.py`** runs the gate checks on each candidate.

The robot used for training is generated by the same code, so the design and the trained robot cannot drift apart.

**Design rules (assumptions, to be measured):**
- installed efficiency 0.85 of bench thrust
- P ∝ T^1.5 (fitted to the JP70 data, exponent 1.54)
- 75% usable pack energy
- 2.5 mΩ/cell pack resistance
- 1.2 mm PETG shells with a 1.35 rib factor
- fasteners 5% of dry mass

### A3. Sizing method: how the robot's height was chosen

1. **The pod size is set by the fan.** Pod OD = fan lip OD + wall + clearance (118 mm for the 90 mm fan). For the "oversized propulsion pods on a slender body" look, the pod OD should be about 13% of height. So each fan class implies a height: 64 mm → 0.55 m, 70 → 0.68, 80 → 0.76, 90 → 0.88, 105 → 0.96.
2. **Segments use standard human anthropometric ratios** (Drillis–Contini), slimmed at the waist and hips for a slender, graceful silhouette.
3. **Mass is built up part by part:** shells from surface area × wall × density, servos chosen by an automatic torque-requirement loop (mass ↔ torque, iterated to a fixed point), wire gauge from current with run lengths from the geometry, and the battery placed in the chest.
4. **Gate metrics** come from the MuJoCo model of each candidate (A4).

**How size changes the physics.** All figures are from each candidate's assembled MuJoCo model in the standing pose.

| Candidate | H (m) | Mass (kg) | Usable thrust/fan (N) | T/W | COM height (m, fraction of H) | Static tip angle (fore-aft) | Fall time constant √(h/g) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| A (64 mm, 6S) | 0.55 | 5.66 | 13.7 | 0.99 | 0.270 (0.49) | 5.4° | 166 ms |
| B (70 mm, 6S) | 0.68 | 6.32 | 19.6 | 1.26 | 0.337 (0.50) | 4.4° | 185 ms |
| C (80 mm, 6S) | 0.76 | 7.43 | 23.2 | 1.27 | 0.372 (0.49) | 4.4° | 195 ms |
| D (90 mm, 8S) | 0.88 | 9.41 | 30.1 | 1.31 | 0.420 (0.48) | 4.6° | 207 ms |
| E2 (90 mm, 12S, 65C) | 0.88 | 10.74 | 43.2 | 1.64 | 0.434 (0.49) | 4.3° | 210 ms |
| F (105 mm, 12S) | 0.96 | 11.65 | 32.5 | 1.14 | 0.466 (0.49) | 4.5° | 218 ms |

- **Mass scales sub-cubically.** From 0.55 to 0.88 m, mass rises 1.9× (exponent about 1.4 in H). The battery, ESCs, flight controller and wiring are fixed-weight items that don't shrink with the robot. Usable thrust per fan rises 3.2× (exponent about 2.4). So thrust/weight *improves* with size until the pack current limit binds. F is the case where it binds: its 105 mm fan is current-limited by the same pack.
- **Joint torque scales with mass × lever.** The gravity torques at the hip and knee grow faster than height. At 0.88 m the hip roll needs 6.0 N·m continuous and 10.0 N·m peak, which forces paired XH540-W270s. Servos become the heaviest subsystem (2.43 kg), and the mass ↔ torque loop in step 3 accounts for that feedback.
- **The COM rises, but standing stability is roughly scale-invariant.** The COM stays at about 49% of H because the proportions are fixed. The pod rim footprint grows with the pod OD, which also scales with H. So the static tip angle stays in the 4–5° band for every candidate. A taller robot falls *more slowly* (the inverted-pendulum time constant grows with √h), giving the controller 44 ms more reaction time at 0.88 m than at 0.55 m.
- **Flight attitude response slows with size.** Rotational inertia grows as m·H², while the pods' moment arms grow only with H. Angular acceleration per newton of differential thrust therefore falls roughly as 1/(m·H). The A4 control-authority check (allocation rank and conditioning, attainable roll/pitch/yaw torque) tests that the chosen size still has enough authority.

### A4. Smoke tests: the M0 feasibility gate

Every candidate is built in MuJoCo and checked **before any training**:

| Check | Threshold | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Installed thrust/weight, nominal | ≥ 1.4 (target 1.8) | Control headroom |
| Thrust/weight worst case | ≥ 1.15, with installed efficiency 0.75 and an end-of-pack cell voltage of 3.5 V | Can still land |
| **Thrust limited by pack current** | battery C-rating × capacity | The fan curve isn't the only limit |
| Trim feasible at the hover pose | wrench residual < 0.1% mg | Hover is possible at all |
| Allocation Jacobian rank / conditioning | rank 6, cond < 100 | 6-DOF control authority from 4 thrusts + gimbals |
| Yaw authority vs reaction-torque uncertainty | ≥ 2× | No counter-rotating units exist |
| Hover endurance | ≥ 60 s | Enough for the M4 cycle |
| Pack / ESC current and voltage | within ratings | |
| Joint servos | continuous and peak margins ≥ 1 | |
| **Static stand** | 3 s physics sim under PD, COM inside the support polygon with ≥ 1 cm margin | Ground support |
| Silhouette | pod ≥ 1.4× thigh diameter; shin pods don't collide | Aesthetic constraint from the brief |

**Sweep iterations**

| Iteration | Change | Outcome |
|---|---|---|
| S1 | Dynamixel-only servos, peak torque used as the continuous requirement, gimbal tilt rate 3 rad/s | The 88 cm, 90 mm 8S design came to **10.43 kg, T/W 1.35**. Leg servos were the largest item (3.6 kg), and the gimbals needed 2× XH540. |
| S2 | Torque model split into continuous (RMS) and peak; added STS3250; gimbal 2 rad/s; pod shells without end caps; added the 90 mm 12S and 105 mm 12S fans; **thrust limited by pack current** | **E (12S + 35C packs) failed: the pack capped the current at 210 A.** E2 (65C packs) **passed at T/W 1.64**. Fans below 90 mm all land at T/W ≤ 1.3. |
| S3 | Hover-pose tuning (grid over shoulder and hip pitch) | First trim put 91% throttle on the arm pods (COM 5 cm ahead of the shin pods) → tuned pose balances throttle at about 60% |

**Result:** only E2 passes (`docs/assets/fig_gate_matrix.png`, `results/m0/pareto_mass.png`). **Smaller is not easier:** fan thrust scales roughly with D², but legs strong enough to carry the pods scale too.

### B2. Environment (`envs/edf_env.py`): one physics core for every task

**Physics beyond rigid bodies:**

| Model | Detail |
|---|---|
| Servos | PD on targets, clipped by a linear torque–speed envelope (peak 0.7 × stall, zero torque at no-load speed when driving), 20 s thermal RMS |
| EDFs | Thrust along the actual gimballed fan axis, applied every 2 ms; first-order spool (0.16 s up / 0.20 s down, randomized); voltage dependence T ∝ V^1.7 |
| Pack | 390 A pack current limit scales all fans; P ∝ T^1.5; SoC, OCV and IR sag |
| Disturbances | Residual reaction torque (all fans spin the same way); ground-effect multiplier 1 + a·exp(−h/D) with **unknown sign** a ∈ [−0.15, +0.10] |
| Contact | 16 pad-tip spheres are the only collision geometry, so no hidden feet |

**Actor observation (87 values):**
- gyro with bias
- gravity vector
- velocity estimate (VIO / optical flow, noisy)
- rangefinder altitude
- position-error estimate
- joint positions and velocities
- last action
- fan-thrust estimate
- pack voltage
- pad contact switches
- a 10-value command vector (velocity, altitude reference, mode flags, gait clock, fan-failure flag)

**Action (22 values):** 12 joint targets around the nominal pose, 6 gimbal angles, 4 throttles. The same layout is used for every task, so policies can warm-start each other.

**Domain randomization (×1):**

| Parameter | Range |
|---|---|
| Fan thrust | −10% / +4% per fan |
| Payload | ±10%, with a ±2 cm COM offset |
| Spool lag | 0.13–0.26 s |
| Servo gain | ±15% |
| Gyro bias | ±0.02 rad/s |
| Initial state of charge | 55–100% |
| Pushes | stand/walk ≤0.3 m/s, flight ≤0.5 m/s |

`dr_level` scales all of it (the "wide DR" runs use 1.5–2).

### B3. Reward design per task

All rewards are scaled by dt. Tracking terms are exp(−e²/σ²); flight tasks use a **fine + coarse** kernel pair (see the lessons).

| Task | Positive terms (weight) | Penalties (weight) | Key design idea |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Stand** | alive 1, upright 1, height 1, stillness 0.5, nominal pose 0.5, pods flat on the floor 0.5 | termination −50, action rate −0.02, thermal overload −0.5, thrust use −1 | Pushes kept below the physical capture limit of an ankle-less stance |
| **Walk** (v4) | velocity tracking 2, yaw tracking 0.8, upright 1, height 0.5, **air time 3**, single support 0.3, **gait-clock swing height 3** | **slip −1** (pad-tip speed while in contact), termination −50, thermal −0.5, pod_flat **0** | A gait clock (0.7 Hz, legs half a cycle apart) passed in the observations defines a 5 cm swing-height profile. The slip penalty uses true pad-tip velocity. |
| **Hover** (v7) | alive 1, altitude 2 (σ 0.08 + 0.4 m), **position 4** (σ 0.22 + 1.0 m), velocity 1, upright 1, yaw rate 0.3, pose 0.3 | ground contact −5, power −0.1, gimbal use −0.05, throttle saturation −0.5, termination −50 | Fine + coarse kernels keep a gradient when far from the setpoint |
| **Transition** (v5) | alive 1, altitude-profile tracking 2.5, vz tracking 1, position 1, upright 1.5, **both pods down in ground phases 1**, idle on ground 0.5 | **impact above 0.3 m/s, squared −8**; **post-landing thrust/weight −4**; power −0.05; termination −50 | A time-indexed stand → climb → hold → descend → stand profile; a strict "unassisted" final stand |
| **Maneuver** | alive 1, heading-frame **velocity tracking 2.5**, altitude 1.5, yaw tracking 0.5, upright 0.5 | ground −5, power −0.05, termination −50 | Commands resampled every 2–4 s (±1 m/s, 25% stop-and-hold) |
| **Failsafe** | = transition, profile switches to a 0.4 m/s descent after the failure | = transition, impact −8 | One random fan dies mid-hover; a failure flag (ESC telemetry) is in the observations |

| Task | Pass criterion | Extra metrics |
|---|---|---|

### B7. Lessons (in the order they were learned)

1. **Test design must respect physics.** With no ankles and disc pods, the no-step capture limit is about 0.19–0.58 m/s. Pushes above that measure nothing. *(stand_v1)*
2. **Narrow tracking kernels lose their gradient off target.** Pair a fine kernel with a coarse one. *(hover_v1)*
3. **Single-pod touchdown is the critical landing moment.** The first pod down becomes a pivot; flying hard through contact tips the robot. *(classical transition)*
4. **Keep the best checkpoint and evaluate deterministically.** Brax evaluates stochastically by default, and the last checkpoint isn't the best. *(hover_v2)*
5. **ROOT CAUSE of the "late PPO collapse": the auto-reset leaked `info`.** Playground's `BraxAutoResetWrapper` (default `full_reset=False`) restores only physics data and observations, so the following carried across episodes:
   - battery SoC (thrust shrank as training went on)
   - the transition step counter (the schedule ran once per env)
   - the DR draw (one per env forever)
   - thermal and push state

   Evaluation was unaffected, which is why early checkpoints looked fine. Fix: `full_reset=True`. All curves afterwards are monotonic (`docs/assets/fig_reset_bug.png`).
6. **Distance metrics can be gamed by shuffling.** Measure gait quality: lift-offs, clearance, slip. *(walk_v1)*
7. **Don't re-score old policies after changing the observation content.** *(walk_v1 re-score)*
8. **Quantify "unsupported" claims.** Contact-only scoring passed a stand carrying 30% of the weight on the fans. *(transition_v2)*
9. **Innocent-looking reward terms can block a behavior.** "Keep pods flat in contact" made lifting a disc foot costly. A fine-tune couldn't escape; a from-scratch run without the term did (9 → 42 mm). *(walk_v3 → v4)*
10. **Warm-starting the policy without its critic costs performance** on an already-good policy. *(maneuver_v2)*
11. **DR width is a design lever with a trade-off.** It roughly doubled stress margins but cost position hold, until the position reward was strengthened. *(hover_v6 → v7)*
12. **Fan failure is a landing problem, not a hover problem.** Sustained 3-fan hover is infeasible, but a trained descent lands 97% of the time. *(failsafe)*
13. **Sensing requirement:** hover without a velocity estimate drifts about 10 m/s, so optical flow is mandatory. *(hover_v8)*
14. **Metric bugs masquerade as policy failures**, three times:
    - the transition scorer had no settling allowance;
    - the maneuver metric compared world-frame velocity with heading-frame commands;
    - a dtype mismatch crashed walk_v1's first launch.

    Always trace a failing trial before changing the policy.
